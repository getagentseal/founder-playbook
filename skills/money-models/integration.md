# money-models - Integration with Other Skills

## The Extended Hormozi Sequence

```
mom-test → 100m-offers → money-models → 100m-leads
   ↓            ↓              ↓              ↓
discovery   packaging   monetization      traffic
                        sequencing
```

Hormozi's own trilogy framing: **100m-offers** = "what do I sell?", **100m-leads** = "how do I get attention?", **money-models** (this) = "how do I get them to buy - fast enough that cash stops being the constraint?"

| Skill | When to use |
|-------|-------------|
| **mom-test** | BEFORE everything - validate the problem, extract dream-outcome language |
| **100m-offers** | Build the single offer that converts (money-models assumes this exists) |
| **money-models** (this) | Sequence offers so a customer self-funds the next customer |
| **100m-leads** | Drive traffic - and money-models sets how much you can afford per lead |

The offer is the *unit*; the money model is the *sequence of units*. If a business has one offer and it converts but growth is capped by cash, that's the gap this skill fills.

## Skill Combinations

### money-models + 100m-offers

The tightest pairing in the repo. Offers builds one irresistible thing; money-models arranges several in time.

| 100m-offers gives you | money-models does with it |
|-----------------------|---------------------------|
| A Grand Slam Offer that converts | Makes it the *attraction* offer (Stage I) |
| Value-stacked components | Peels components off as feature downsells; adds some as continuity bonuses |
| A guarantee | Treats it as a removable feature (drop it → lower price) or a Pay-Later conditional guarantee |
| Premium pricing | Uses it as the anchor; the "main" offer is priced below it |

Sequence: build the converting offer first, confirm it works on warm traffic, *then* wrap the money model around it.

### money-models + 100m-leads

The money model **determines how much you can afford per lead.** The more cash a customer produces in the first 30 days, the higher a CAC you can profitably pay - which unlocks channels that were previously too expensive.

```
Weak money model → low affordable CAC → only free/cheap channels work
Strong money model → high affordable CAC → paid ads, affiliates,
                     agencies all become affordable
```

Run this skill *before* scaling spend: more leads into a leaky money model just loses money faster.

### money-models + monetizing-innovation

Both touch pricing, from opposite ends.

| Aspect | monetizing-innovation | money-models |
|--------|-----------------------|--------------|
| Question | What should the price and tiers *be*? | In what *sequence* do we present offers? |
| Basis | Willingness-to-pay research, segments | Cash-flow timing, conversion tactics |
| Horizon | Strategic (product + price design) | Tactical (this-quarter cash) |

**Named conflict - discounting.** monetizing-innovation warns that discounting trains customers to wait and erodes willingness-to-pay. money-models leans heavily on free/discount attraction offers. Resolution: attraction offers discount the **first transaction only**, never the core recurring price - and Hormozi's own rule reinforces it ("never drop the price on the same thing; downsell features or payment terms instead"). Use monetizing-innovation to set the real price; use money-models to design the *first-purchase* hook that doesn't touch it.

### money-models + traction

Traction picks *which* channel via the Bullseye Framework; the money model sets the economics that make a channel viable. A channel isn't "bad" in the abstract - it's too expensive for a *weak* money model and fine for a strong one. Fix the model, re-run the channel math.

### money-models + influence

Cialdini's principles are the machinery inside these offers - which is exactly why the ethics matter.

| Offer mechanic | Influence principle |
|----------------|---------------------|
| Win Your Money Back, Trial With Penalty | Commitment & consistency, loss aversion |
| Bonuses, "surprise and delight," free orientations | Reciprocity |
| Giveaway winners, "8 of 10 chose premium" | Social proof |
| Deadlines, one-time rollover, limited entries | Scarcity |
| Anchor Upsell, "the gasp" | Anchoring / contrast |

Use **influence** to understand *why* they work - and to keep them on the ethical side of the line (see Conflicts below).

### money-models + spin-selling

In B2B / high-touch sales, the offer sequence has to sit inside a conversation, not a checkout page. SPIN structures the talk; money-models supplies the moments.

| SPIN stage | money-models moment |
|------------|---------------------|
| Situation / Problem questions | Surface the problem the attraction offer solved and the *next* one |
| Implication questions | Build the need the upsell answers ("you can't have X without Y") |
| Need-payoff | Present the upsell; if declined, run the downsell ladder |

Downsell laddering maps naturally onto B2B objection-handling; the temperature check ("1-10") is a SPIN-friendly commitment probe. Just slow it down - a 9-offer payment ladder in one call reads as pushy in enterprise.

### money-models + lean-startup

"Perfect one offer at a time" is small-batch thinking. Each offer is a hypothesis; run one until reliable (Build-Measure-Learn) before adding the next. "Raise price in stages until nos outweigh yeses" is innovation accounting applied to pricing. Don't launch a four-stage model at once - that's a big-batch release that breaks the business.

## Conflicts and Tensions

### Conflict 1: Aggressive urgency/penalty tactics vs ethical-influence lines

**Hormozi:** trials-with-penalty, cancellation fees, "make cancellation findable but not easy," one-time-only rollovers.
**Cialdini / trust economics:** manufactured scarcity and coercive commitment erode trust and invite dark-pattern enforcement.

**Resolution:** Hormozi draws his own floor - refund anyone who doesn't want you to have their money, hard selling is for weak products, be transparent. Keep penalties *aligned to real value* (fees that fund genuine onboarding), disclose auto-renewal up front, and make cancellation genuinely easy. If a tactic only works because the customer *didn't understand it*, it fails both Cialdini's test and the law.

### Conflict 2: Premium-price philosophy (Offers) vs free/discount attraction (here)

**100m-offers:** charge premium; price signals value; never compete on price.
**money-models:** lead with free, $1, giveaways, buy-X-get-Y-free.

**Resolution:** they don't actually collide. **Attraction offers discount the FIRST transaction only, never the core price.** The premium price stays intact for the upsell, the continuity, and every future quote. The hook is a customer-acquisition cost, not a repricing of the product.

### Conflict 3: 30-day payback vs SaaS / long-cycle reality

**Hormozi:** recover acquire + serve cost within 30 days.
**Reality:** SaaS payback is 6-12+ months; enterprise deals take quarters.

**Resolution:** treat 30 days as a **north star, not a gate.** Pull cash forward where the model allows (annual prepay, onboarding/setup fees, bulk blocks), and accept a longer honest payback where it doesn't. The *sequencing logic* (attract → upsell → downsell → continuity, one at a time) transfers even when the 30-day number doesn't.

### Conflict 4: "Everybody buys something" (downsell everything) vs qualification

**Hormozi:** convert every no into a smaller yes.
**Reality:** some prospects should be let go; over-converting fills the base with low-fit, high-complaint customers.

**Resolution:** Hormozi partly agrees ("if someone won't leave a card and do the work, I won't sell them"). Downsell to fit, not to force. A customer converted past their real budget or intent churns, refunds, and reviews badly - erasing the marginal sale.

### Conflict 5: Continuity as recurring value vs continuity as lock-in

**Reward-based** continuity (bonuses, discounts) aligns incentives. **Fee/penalty-based** continuity (waived-fee, cancellation fees) can shade into handcuffing.

**Resolution:** Hormozi himself prefers reward structures and flags ">5% wanting to cancel early = look into it." If people want out, the product is the problem; a fee that traps them just converts churn into resentment and bad reviews.

## When money-models is the Wrong Tool

- **No offer that converts yet:** Use **100m-offers** (or **mom-test** if pre-validation). Sequencing can't rescue an offer nobody wants.
- **No traffic source:** Use **traction** / **100m-leads**. A money model needs leads to run on.
- **Pure positioning / category confusion:** Use **obviously-awesome**.
- **Strategic pricing / tier design:** Use **monetizing-innovation**.
- **Enterprise / committee sales:** Use **spin-selling**; rapid downsell laddering doesn't fit long cycles.
- **Regulated or trust-first categories:** the penalty/auto-renew mechanics create legal and reputational risk that can outweigh the cash gain.
- **Genuinely one-time, non-repeatable purchase:** no continuity to add - maximize the single basket instead.

## The Dependency Map

```
Discovery layer:    mom-test → spin-selling
                       ↓
Strategy layer:     obviously-awesome / monetizing-innovation / blue-ocean
                       ↓
Offer layer:        100m-offers  (build the converting unit)
                       ↓
Monetization layer: money-models (THIS - sequence the units for cash)
                       ↓
Traffic layer:      traction → 100m-leads (afford more per lead)
                       ↓
Influence layer:    influence (the mechanics inside every offer)
```

money-models sits between offer construction and traffic. It assumes a converting offer exists above it and sets the economics for the traffic layer below it. If the offer layer is unresolved, go up the stack first.
